Privacy notice

What Kisapmata collects about you, why, who can see it, and how to exercise your rights under the Data Privacy Act of 2012 (Republic Act No. 10173).

Who is responsible

Pampanga State University operates Kisapmata for International Eye Center, Robinsons Starmills, City of San Fernando, Pampanga. You can reach the clinic at internationaleyecenter@yahoo.com or 0920 909 9937. Questions about your data go to our Data Protection Officer, [Data Protection Officer name], at [DPO email address].

What we collect

For patients: your name, date of birth (from which your age is worked out), gender, contact number, the reason for your visit and which eye it concerns, your answers to a short eye-health questionnaire (for example diabetes, high blood pressure, contact lens use, past eye surgery or injury, medicines and allergies), measurements taken before your examination (your vision and eye pressure), slit-lamp photographs of your eyes taken during an examination, each marked as the right or the left eye, the screening result and heatmap for each photograph, your ophthalmologist's assessment and notes, and your appointments. If the clinic gives you an account, also your email address.

For staff: your name, email address, role, an optional profile picture, and a log of when you sign in. For ophthalmologists, the PRC licence number and expiry date if you enter them.

For every account: whether you have confirmed your email address.

We do not collect payment details, location, contacts, or anything for advertising.

Why we use it

Only to provide and record your eye care: registering your visit, scheduling appointments and reminding you of them, screening photographs, keeping your medical record, and letting you see your own results. We also use your email address to confirm that the account really belongs to you before it can be used.

Health information is sensitive personal information, so we record your consent (or your parent's or guardian's, if you are under 18) when you are registered.

How the screening works

A machine-learning model (three convolutional neural networks whose scores are averaged) checks each photograph for 13 conditions of the front of the eye, such as cataract, keratitis and pterygium. Each condition gets its own score and is reported when that score reaches its threshold, so one photograph can show more than one. A heatmap shows which part of the image influenced each condition. If it finds no condition and cannot call the eye normal either, it names none and leaves the result to the ophthalmologist.

When your ophthalmologist releases a result to you, you see the conditions they confirmed and the model's score for each. You do not see the heatmap.

It is a screening aid, not a diagnosis, and it can be wrong. An ophthalmologist reviews every result and makes the clinical decision. The model never decides your treatment.

Who can see it

Clinic staff, according to their role: the ophthalmologist sees clinical records and screening results, the secretary sees registration and scheduling details, and patients see only their own records.

You see a screening result only after your ophthalmologist has reviewed it and released it to you. What you see is the finding they confirmed, not the model's own output.

Your eye-health answers and the measurements taken before your examination are seen by the clinic staff treating you. The record shows who entered each answer and when, and earlier answers are kept when they change. You can see and update your own answers in your portal.

We use these service providers to run the app:

  • Supabase: stores the database, sign-in accounts and scan images (region: Singapore, AWS ap-southeast-1).
  • Google (Gmail): sends account emails (a temporary password and a 6-digit verification code), appointment confirmations and reminders.
  • Hugging Face: runs our server and the screening model. Your eye photographs and the details sent with each scan pass through that server to be analysed. It saves them to Supabase and does not keep its own copy.
  • Vercel: hosts the web version of Kisapmata. It receives your IP address and browser details when you open the site, as any website host does. No patient records are stored there.
  • On the web version, your browser downloads part of the app's display engine from Google's servers (gstatic.com), and sometimes a font for characters the app's own fonts do not include. This shares your IP address with Google.

These providers' servers are outside the Philippines, so your data is transferred abroad to them. Each receives only what it needs for its task, and every connection to them is encrypted.

We do not sell your data or share it with anyone else unless the law requires it.

The clinic's Gmail account

Kisapmata sends its emails from the clinic's Gmail account, kisapmata.admin@gmail.com. Its permission on that account is limited to sending the emails listed above. It cannot read the mailbox, and it does not access anyone else's Google account.

Cookies and storage on your device

Kisapmata does not use advertising or analytics cookies, and does not track you across other sites. It stores your sign-in session and a few settings (such as whether heatmaps are generated) on your device or in your browser's local storage. These are needed for the app to work, so there is nothing to opt out of. Signing out removes the session; on the doctor's workspace it also clears scan images cached on that device.

Screening reports

When the ophthalmologist saves your screening report, a PDF of it is stored with your medical record, in the same private storage as your eye photographs. Only the ophthalmologist and you can open it. The clinic records each time a report is saved, opened, printed, or saved as a copy, and by whom.

A printed or saved copy is outside Kisapmata's protection. Clinic staff are reminded that it contains sensitive information before they save one.

How we protect it

Every account is locked until its owner enters the code sent to their email address, so an account cannot be opened with an address that does not exist or belongs to someone else. Every request to our server must carry a valid sign-in. The app opens scan images through links that expire after an hour.

How long we keep it

Medical records: [Retention period set by the clinic records policy]. A record of each staff sign-in (email address and time) is also kept.

Children

Patients under 18 are registered only with the consent of a parent or guardian, whose name is recorded with the registration.

Your rights

You have the right to be informed, to access your data, to object to its processing, to have it corrected, to have it erased or blocked, to data portability, to be indemnified for damages, and to file a complaint with the National Privacy Commission.

To correct your name, date of birth or gender, ask the front desk: they are printed on your reports, so the clinic checks them before changing them. You can change your contact number yourself in Settings.

To have your data erased or blocked, write to [DPO email address]. The clinic reviews each request and tells you the outcome by email; a refusal always comes with the reason. Your examinations and screening results may have to be kept for a period set by law.
